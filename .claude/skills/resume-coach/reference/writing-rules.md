# Writing rules (resume and cover letter)

These were refined through a real job search. They apply to every document the kit produces.

## Bullets
- **Lead with a strong, specific verb** for what *they* did: Built, Led, Cut, Designed,
  Negotiated, Trained, Launched, Automated. Never "Responsible for", "Helped with",
  "Worked on", "Assisted in" (if they truly assisted, say what part they owned).
- **What they did -> the result -> why it mattered.** "Automated the monthly no-show report
  in Python, saving 12 analyst hours a month" beats "Created reports".
- **Real numbers when real.** Team size, money, time saved, %, volume, count of users,
  sites, customers. Ask for them. Never make them up. A credible before/after
  ("from 3 days to 20 minutes") beats an extreme ratio ("99.3% faster").
- **Concrete nouns.** Name the actual system, tool, customer type, team. Not "various
  stakeholders" but "finance and clinic managers".
- **One to two lines** each on the page. If it wraps to three, it's two bullets or needs cutting.
- **Most relevant bullet first** within each job (relevance to the target jobs, not chronology).
- **No two bullets that say the same thing.** Merge them.

## Banned or suspicious words
Replace each with a fact: passionate, dedicated, hard-working, detail-oriented, team player,
results-driven, dynamic, proven, strong, skilled, experienced, motivated, enthusiastic,
innovative, synergy, leverage (as a verb), spearheaded (overused), utilize (say "use"),
"various", "multiple", "numerous", "successfully" (redundant), "etc."

## Punctuation and style
- No em-dashes in prose. Use a comma, a period, or "to". (En dash in date ranges is fine:
  "Mar 2021 – Present".)
- No semicolons. Split into two sentences or restructure.
- No lazy parentheticals. If it matters, say it in the sentence.
- Consistent tense: past tense for past roles, present tense for the current role.
- No first person on the resume ("I", "my"). Cover letters use first person naturally.
- One date style throughout: "Mar 2021 – Present". Month abbreviations, en dash.

## Summary (top of the resume)
2-3 sentences. Who they are professionally *for the target jobs*, their strongest evidence
(one or two numbers), and what they're moving toward if it's a pivot. No adjective soup.
Bad: "Results-driven professional with a passion for data." Good: "Operations analyst moving
into product analytics. Six years turning messy operational data into decisions: built the
forecasting model that cut overtime 22% across 14 clinics..."

## What to leave off (default, US)
- Roles shorter than 6 months, unless they were contracts or the person insists. (Short stints
  invite "why did you leave" on paper. Easy to explain in person, costly on paper.)
- "References available upon request". Objective statements. Photo, birth date, marital status,
  full street address (city and state are enough).
- Jobs older than ~15 years unless they're directly relevant. They can go in a one-line
  "Earlier experience" entry.
- Skills nobody would hire for today (Microsoft Word, email) unless the job asks for them.

## Length
- Resume: 1 page for under ~8 years of experience, 2 pages max otherwise.
- Cover letter: 1 page, 3-4 short paragraphs, roughly 250-350 words.
